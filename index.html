What businesses actually face

Six ways fraud and cyber risk hit small businesses.

Payment & banking-change fraud

A supplier or sub "updates" their banking details by email, and the next payment goes straight to a criminal.

Email compromise (BEC)

Someone gets into a real email account — yours, a supplier's, or a client's — and uses it to redirect payments or pull information, from an address you already trust.

Fake or altered invoices

A convincing invoice arrives for work that was never done, or a real invoice gets intercepted and the account number is quietly changed before it reaches you.

Phishing & credential theft

A text or email made to look like your bank, Microsoft 365, or a supplier tricks someone on your team into typing in a password — that's all it takes to get in.

Vendor & subcontractor impersonation

Someone poses as a trade you already work with to request an urgent payment, a password, or access to a shared project folder.

Ransomware

Your files, drawings, and project data get locked up and held for payment — one infected laptop on a job site can be enough to spread it.

Sources: Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre · Royal Canadian Mounted Police
